Hard money loans, explained by people who use them

Hard money is a short-term loan secured by real estate and approved on the property and the plan. The name comes from the hard asset behind it. It has nothing to do with being hard to get.

Glossary

The terms on your term sheet

ARV
After-repair value. What the property should be worth once the work is done.
LTV / LTC
Loan-to-value and loan-to-cost. The ratios that cap how large the loan can be.
Rehab holdback
Renovation money set aside at closing and released as work is completed.
Draw
A release of holdback funds after an inspection confirms the work.
Points
A fee stated as a percentage of the loan. One point is one percent.
Interest-only
You pay interest during the loan and the principal at payoff.
Exit strategy
How the loan gets repaid: sale or refinance.
BPO
Broker price opinion. A value estimate that can stand in for a full appraisal.
Questions

Hard money questions

Is hard money a last resort?

No. Experienced investors choose it because speed and certainty win deals. A seller will often take a lower price from a buyer who can close in days.

Why do you always ask about my exit?

Because the exit is how the loan gets repaid. If you cannot say whether you will sell or refinance, and roughly when, the deal is not ready to fund.

How do you decide the ARV?

From recent sales of renovated homes near the property, finished to the standard your scope of work describes.

What property types do you finance?

Non-owner-occupied investment property: single-family homes, two-to-four unit buildings and light commercial.

Is hard money more expensive than a bank loan?

Per month, yes. You are paying for speed, flexibility and a loan on a house a bank would not touch in its current condition. The question is what the deal earns after the cost of capital, which is what our calculator shows.
